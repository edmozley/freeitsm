## What this release is about

**Every module now works on a phone.** This release brings the last two along — **Reporting** (the landing page, System logs, the Intune dashboard and the guide) and the **System Wiki** (browse, search, database tables, scan management and the three detail pages) — and finishes the **self-service portal**, which is the half of FreeITSM most of the people you support will only ever see on a phone. Nothing changes on a desktop.

Several of those pages were worse than merely cramped. **Both user guides could not be scrolled at all** — seven thousand pixels each, of which only the first screenful was reachable — and five of the seven System Wiki pages had nothing able to scroll either. The Reporting landing page was showing one and a half of its three links, with the first one entirely off the left of the screen and no way to reach it. And on the portal dashboard, the only way to open a ticket was to hit a seventeen-pixel line of text. Asset Management gets a round of polish in the same spirit: the buttons for re-assigning an asset had been drawn straight over the email address of the person holding it.

This is a patch release. There is nothing to configure and nothing new to learn.

## Improvements

- **Reporting on a phone.** The four-item navigation moves into the menu behind the ☰ button, and the two log tables and the Intune device list become a card per row, with each value printed against the column heading it needs — an IP address, a user agent, an attachment list, or a bare **Yes** under **Encrypted**.

- **The System Wiki on a phone.** The folder tree moves into a full-screen panel opened by a **Folders** button, because a 280px tree beside the file list left the list 80px of the screen. Tapping a folder that has sub-folders opens them and keeps the panel up; tapping one that does not closes it and shows you the files. The file list, the database-tables list and the scan history each become a card per row, and a file's page keeps the three of its six tables that already read well as tables.

- **The Intune dashboard is usable on a small screen.** Tapping a figure — compliant, encrypted, stale, newly enrolled — used to open a six-column table inside a small box floating in the middle of the screen, so every row needed a sideways drag and the Export and Close buttons ran off the edge. It now fills the screen, each device is a card, and **Export CSV** and **Close** sit on a row of their own. The five figures above are two to a row rather than five, which gives back most of a screen's height they were spending on labels wrapping to three lines.

- **An asset's actions sit along the bottom of a phone screen.** **View History**, **Custody** and **Print label** become icons in a bar pinned to the bottom, where a thumb already is — the same treatment knowledge articles, contracts and the ticket reading pane already use. That hands their row back to the top of the pane, which is where the asset's own name and the person holding it are, and every list in the pane gained the bar's height as padding so its last row can still be read.

- **The Watchtower dashboard gives a phone its screen back.** The top of the page was three stacked rows — the heading **Attention Overview**, then **Mine / My team / Everyone**, then the update time and **Refresh** — which spent about 120px before a single figure. The heading is gone on a phone, where it was the only thing on the page that said nothing, and the other five now share one line: the scope buttons lose padding they do not need to stay tappable, and Refresh keeps its arrow and drops its word. The desktop is exactly as it was.

## Fixes

- **The Re-assign and Remove buttons were sitting on top of the assigned person's email address.** On a phone, the bar naming who holds an asset gave its details a box 169 pixels wide and put 297 pixels of text in it — the name, the email address and the date it was assigned, all on one line that refused to wrap. The email ran on underneath **Re-assign** and the date underneath **Remove**, so two of the three facts on that bar were unreadable, and tapping the end of the email pressed a button instead. The three now sit one per line at the full width, with the buttons on a row of their own beneath them.

- **The task board became unreachable on a phone.** The switch between **Board** and **List** lives in the Tasks sidebar, which is hidden on a small screen — which cost nothing while the page always opened on the board. Once Tasks began remembering that choice, anyone whose remembered view was **List** arrived at the list on their phone with no control anywhere on the page to get back to the board. And because a list has nothing that scrolls sideways, a sideways swipe fell through to the browser's own back gesture, so it looked as though swiping navigated to whichever page you had visited before. The switch is now a two-button row across the top of the board on a phone. The board itself was never broken.

- **The Reporting landing page was showing one and a half cards out of three.** Its three links sat side by side on a row wider than a phone, centred, so the row overhung on both sides: **System Logs was entirely off the left of the screen with no way to scroll to it**, and Intune was cut off on the right. They now stack and take the full width.

- **The Reporting guide could not be scrolled on a phone.** All six sections were there — seven thousand pixels of them — inside a screen that showed the first seven hundred and refused to move. The numbered contents strip along the top was tappable and did nothing at the same time.

- **Five of the seven System Wiki pages could not be scrolled either.** Each is built as a full-height panel sized to the screen, so anything past the first screenful is unreachable. The browse page was the worst — a fifth of it simply not gettable-to — and the other four would be as bad on an installation that has actually run a scan, since their tables grow with the codebase.

- **The System Wiki's collapsing left panel was a dead end on a phone.** With the left panel set to appear **on hover**, the whole folder tree was sealed inside a 16px strip that a finger cannot open. It is now always reachable on a small screen, whichever setting you use, and the setting is unchanged on a desktop.

- **The System Wiki's scan history was seven unlabelled values in a column.** Each scan's date, status, duration and three counts were stacked one per line with nothing to say which figure was which. The date now leads, the status keeps its badge, and every figure carries its heading.

- **The self-service portal's Help & Guide could not be scrolled on a phone.** All eleven sections were there — seven thousand pixels of them — behind a screen that showed the first seven hundred and refused to move, and every numbered link along the top did nothing when tapped. Both are fixed, and the highlight now follows as you read.

- **Opening a ticket from the portal dashboard meant hitting a 17px line of text.** The rows themselves are not tappable, so the reference and the subject were the only targets, and a rule meant to give them a comfortable height had never worked. The subject now takes a proper full-width row.

- **"Your requests" on the portal dashboard was laid out as though it were the ticket list.** Both are drawn with the same markup but have different columns, so a request's approval status was being styled as though it were a ticket's subject — the pill on its own line, the ticket reference stranded below it and the date pushed alongside.

- **"Reply" in the email import log was drawn as a badge with no colour in it.** The log marks each imported email as either **New Ticket** or **Reply**, and only the first was given a tone, so **Reply** appeared as an empty pill — the same size and shape as its neighbour with nothing to make it look like a label. This was the same on the desktop and is corrected everywhere.

## Upgrading

1. Pull the new code, or rebuild the Docker image.
2. Hard-refresh your browser (Ctrl-F5) — this release changes stylesheets and scripts, and a cached copy will look unchanged.

No database changes, so **Database Verification** is not required this time.

## Rollback

Safe — nothing was removed from the database, so you can go back to 1.1.1 at any time.
