## What this release is about

**A form can now do something when it is used, rather than just storing an answer.** Open any form, choose the new **What happens next** tab, and say what should happen: raise a ticket with the priority, queue, type and assignee you choose; send an email; call a webhook; create a task; add a note. There are three separate moments - when the form is **submitted**, when a request is **approved**, and when one is **rejected** - and you can put as many actions in each as you like, or none at all. It is what turns a form from a way of collecting answers into the front door of a service request.

**There is a fix in here that matters more than it sounds.** If a form was set to need approval before a ticket was raised, editing that form was quietly removing the approval requirement. The catalogue offers the newest version of a form, so from the moment somebody pressed **Save as new version**, requests that needed a manager's sign-off went straight through without one - and, because approval is also what raises the ticket, those requests stopped becoming tickets at all. Nothing announced either change, and the form looked exactly as it had before. **If you use catalogue approvals, please read the Upgrading section below** - this one cannot be repaired automatically.

**And approvals were never telling anybody anything.** A request waiting for sign-off was supposed to be able to notify its approver, and a decision was supposed to be able to tell the requester the outcome. Neither could ever happen: the three events involved were being announced by the product but were missing from the list of events a rule is allowed to listen for, so there was no way to build the rule and no way to notice the gap. An approver simply had to think to go and look. All three now work.

This is a minor release. There is one thing to do after upgrading, and one thing to check.

## New

- **What happens next.** A third tab in the form designer, beside Fields and Preview, holding three sections: **when submitted**, **when approved**, **when rejected**. Add as many actions to each as you want and move them into the order they should run. Anywhere you can type, you can drop in what somebody actually answered by writing the field label in double braces - so `{{submission.fields.Device type}}` puts their answer into the ticket title, and a hardware request stops arriving with the same subject every time. It is the same automation engine the Workflows module uses, so a form's actions show up in the same run log as everything else, and they behave identically whether the form was filled in on the portal, by an analyst, or through the API.

- **One action can use what the last one produced.** Raise a ticket, then send an email containing `{{last.ticket_id}}`, and the person gets their ticket number in the confirmation. That pairing - the most obvious two things anybody wants to do at once - was simply not possible before, in one rule or in two.

- **An email can be sent when there is no ticket yet.** Choose which mailbox to send from and the ticket becomes optional, which is how a form acknowledges a submission before anything has been raised.

## Improvements

- **A ticket raised from a form is now the same ticket however it was raised.** Two different parts of FreeITSM could turn a form into a ticket and they disagreed. Approving a catalogue request produced a good one: the requester matched to their actual account, the company taken from that account, and the answers laid out as a table. A workflow's **Create a ticket** produced a weaker one: the requester matched by the text of an email address, and no company at all - which on a multi-company install left the ticket unattributed and needing triage. Both now go through the same code, so the good ticket is the only kind there is. Anything you configure still wins; the form only fills in what you left blank. Leave the body empty on purpose and you get the submitted answers as a table.

- **Approving or rejecting a request can use the answers.** Those two moments carried the approver's comment and the ticket that was raised, but not what had been asked for - so a rule could tell somebody their request was approved without being able to say which laptop, or which start date.

- **Every ticket raised from a form records where it came from**, so a surprising ticket can be traced back to the form and the rule that produced it.

## Fixes

- **Editing a form that needed approval silently removed the approval requirement.** The setting and the named approver were not carried to the new version, so an edited form went back to letting requests straight through - and stopped raising tickets for them. See **Upgrading**.

- **Nobody was ever told that a request was waiting for them.** The three approval events could not be listened for at all, so no notification rule could be built on them.

- **A rule could not be restricted to a single form.** Building automation for a form offered **Form** as something to match on and then showed an empty list with nothing in it to choose - so the only rule you could actually save ran for *every* form on the system. Anyone wanting "raise a ticket when the hardware request form arrives" got a rule that also fired for the staff survey. It now lists your forms by name, offering the current version of each.

- **A ticket raised from a form described itself wrongly.** Its first line claimed the request came from the catalogue even when no catalogue or approval was involved, and repeated the ticket's own subject back at you instead of naming the form that produced it.

## Upgrading

1. Pull the new code, or rebuild the Docker image.
2. Sign in as an administrator and run **System → Database Verification**. This adds one column to the forms table.
3. Hard-refresh your browser (Ctrl-F5).

⚠️ **If you use catalogue approvals, check your forms.** Any approval-gated form that was edited into a new version before this release has lost its approval setting and its named approver, and there is nothing to recover them from - the values were dropped at the moment of editing. Open each form that should need sign-off, confirm **Requires approval** is still on and the right person is named, and set them again if not. Forms that have never been edited into a new version are unaffected.

Nothing else needs configuring. Existing forms behave exactly as they did until you open the new tab and choose something - including still raising a ticket on approval, so nothing you already rely on changes by itself.

## Rollback

Safe - nothing was removed from the database, so you can go back to 1.2.0. The one new column is simply ignored by the older code.
