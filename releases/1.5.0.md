## What this release is about

This release came almost entirely from one email. A prospective user wrote in with four things they wanted, and 1.5.0 is all four of them, built.

You can now categorise tickets - and, just as usefully, record what a ticket **turned out** to be when it closes, which is how you find out that twelve of your thirty-seven "printer problems" were really the network. You can add cloud platforms like Xero or Canva to the Software module by hand, which finally gives their cost, seat count and renewal date somewhere to live. Those renewals now show up on your dashboard and in your calendar instead of sitting on a page nobody opens. And if you support several schools, charities or clients from one service desk, there is a single board across all of them.

Nothing here changes what you see until you switch it on. Every new field starts turned off.

## New features

- **Ticket categories.** A ticket type says what kind of thing this is; a category says what it is *about*. Build the list under **Tickets → Settings → Categories**, with sub-categories up to three levels deep if you want them and none of it compulsory. A category can be tied to one ticket type so "User onboarding" is only ever offered on a service request, or left free to appear on all of them. A ticket carries one category, deliberately, so a report of your categories always adds up to the number of tickets you actually had. You can offer a short, plain-English version of the list to requesters in the self-service portal while analysts see the whole tree, and retiring a category leaves it on the tickets that already have it.

- **Category at close.** The same list, asked again when a ticket finishes: what it was reported as, versus what it turned out to be. Most of the time the analyst just confirms it. It earns its place on the rest - the person raising the ticket is guessing, and the analyst who fixed it knows.

- **Resolution codes.** A separate field recording *how* a ticket ended rather than what it was about: "Fixed remotely", "Training given", "No fault found". Ten sensible ones are there from the start and you can change them all. This is the one that most quickly changes what you do - a fifth of your tickets coming back as "Training given" is a documentation problem, not an IT one.

- **Cloud platforms in the Software module.** The inventory agent finds what is installed on your machines, which is no help at all for anything that lives in a browser. **Software → Add application** lets you enter one yourself. That matters more than it sounds: a licence has to belong to an application, so until now there was nowhere to record what a cloud subscription costs, how many seats it has, or when it renews. If the agent later finds one of your manual entries genuinely installed somewhere, it attaches the machines without touching a word you wrote.

- **Software renewals where you will actually see them.** A new **Software** card on the Watchtower dashboard counts licences renewing in the next 30 and 90 days plus notice periods running out - the same three windows the Contracts card uses, so you can read them together. Renewals can also be written into the calendar, and you get two entries per licence: the renewal date, and the **notice deadline** worked out from the notice period. The second is the one that actually costs you if you miss it. Choose where they appear under **Software → Settings → Renewals**.

- **One board across every company.** If you run several companies from one service desk, the company switcher now has an **All companies** option. Every ticket you are allowed to see appears in one list with its company on the row, and anything that arrived by email or the portal without being routed yet is marked **Unrouted** so it stands out. It grants nothing new - "all" means every company you could already reach one at a time. Each entity still stays separate where it matters: open a ticket and its type, category and resolution list are the ones belonging to *that* company, replies still go out from the mailbox the ticket arrived at, and raising a ticket asks which company it is for.

## Improvements

- **Changing a ticket's type clears a category that belonged to the old one**, and says so on screen, rather than leaving a mismatched pair on the ticket that nobody notices until a report is read. A category not tied to any type survives the change, and the clear is recorded in the ticket's history.

- **Categories, category at close and resolution codes can all be charted** on the ticket dashboard. The two category charts roll up to the top level, and tickets with nothing recorded are counted as "Not categorised" rather than quietly left out.

- **The application count now reads as part of the Software heading** - "Software Inventory - 1,500 applications" - instead of floating at the far right of the toolbar.

- **The in-app help covers all of it.** Tickets, Software, Watchtower and the self-service portal help pages have been brought up to date in the same release, rather than lagging behind it.

## Fixes

- **The two switches on Tickets → Settings → Time tracking rendered as plain checkboxes** instead of proper toggles.

- **The Software inventory columns jumped about while you typed in the search box**, because the widest publisher in whatever currently matched was setting the width of the whole table. The columns now stay put, including when a search returns nothing.

## Upgrading

1. Pull the new code, or rebuild the Docker image.
2. Sign in as an administrator and run **System → Database Verification**.
3. Hard-refresh your browser (Ctrl-F5).

Rollback: safe - this release only adds to the database.

## Worth knowing

Categories, category at close and resolution codes **all start switched off**, so nothing changes on your ticket screen until you turn them on under **Tickets → Settings → Categories**. Build your list first, then switch on the fields you want. Each can be set differently for each company.

FreeITSM deliberately ships **no** categories at all. A category tree is the one thing every organisation has to own, and a pre-seeded one is just somebody else's wrong answer that you have to delete first. Resolution codes are the exception - those are near enough universal, so ten are there from the start.
