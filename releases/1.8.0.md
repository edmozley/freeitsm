## What this release is about

**People.** A person in FreeITSM has always had somewhere to record a job title, an office, a phone number and who they report to — and on the screen most analysts actually use, there was no way to see any of it, let alone type it in. That is fixed, and then taken two steps further.

The big one: **FreeITSM can now read a CardDAV address book.** If your customers already exist somewhere with their phone numbers kept current by somebody, you no longer type them in a second time and watch the two lists drift apart. Change a mobile number where you already maintain it, and the next import changes it here. It reads and never writes, so it is safe to point at a book you rely on for other things.

The second: **a customer can correct their own details** from the self-service portal. A phone number that has changed is a phone number you cannot reach, and routing every correction through a ticket means most of them never get made.

There is also **copy and paste on the staff rota** — a shift, or an entire week — and a good deal of in-app help that should have existed already: six System screens that had no guide at all now have one, and the CardDAV import arrived with its own.

## Security

**Only affects installations running more than one company.** If you run a single company, nothing here applies to you.

An analyst restricted to some of your companies could set a person's **manager** to somebody in a company they are not allowed to see, and the manager's name was then displayed back to them on the Assets people screen. Two separate gaps made that possible: the manager field was checked only for "does this person exist anywhere on the system", and the query that fetches a manager's name was not restricted to the companies the analyst can reach.

Both are fixed. The manager must now be somebody the analyst can already see, and a manager outside their reach simply shows as no manager rather than being named. The refusal also no longer distinguishes "that person is in another company" from "that person does not exist", so the field can no longer be used to discover who exists elsewhere on your system.

**Affected:** every release up to and including 1.7.0. The Assets people screen has shown a manager's name since 1.0.0. **What you need to do:** upgrade. Nothing else — no setting to change, and no data to correct. It is worth knowing that this needed a manager link *across* two companies to exist in the first place, which nothing creates on its own; on the installation where this was found, there were none.

## New features

- **Import your customers from a CardDAV address book.** Add one under **System → Authentication**, press **Test connection**, and FreeITSM asks your server which address books it can see and lists them. Choose a book, then choose which of its contacts you want — everyone in it, or only the **groups** or **tags** you tick, from a list the server itself provides rather than a name you have to type correctly. **Preview** tells you exactly what a real import would do without changing anything, and a **History** tab records every run. It brings across name, email, job title, department, office, phone and mobile.

  Nobody signs in through this — an address book is not an identity provider and grants nobody access to anything. It is **one way and read-only**: FreeITSM never adds, edits or deletes anything in your address book. Contacts become the people who raise tickets, never analysts. Built and tested against Baïkal; any CardDAV server should work, including Nextcloud, ownCloud and Radicale. Requested in [issue #133](https://github.com/edmozley/freeitsm/issues/133) — see [CardDAV contact sync](https://github.com/edmozley/freeitsm/wiki/CardDAV-Contact-Sync) for the full setup.

- **It shares the safety rules the directory import already had.** Nobody is ever deleted — the worst an import can do is mark somebody as having left. A run that finds far fewer contacts than last time **stops without changing anything**, because pointing at the wrong address book looks exactly like your whole customer base resigning. And somebody absent from one import is not treated as a leaver; it takes three runs. A group renamed on your server stops the run rather than quietly retiring everybody in it.

- **A person's contact details, everywhere you look them up.** Job title, department, office, phone, mobile, employee ID and who they report to now appear on **Tickets → Users** as well as **Assets → Users**, so it no longer matters which module you came in through. All optional. Two are worth filling in for reasons beyond neatness: **office** tells you which site somebody is at before you set off looking for them, and **employee ID** is what a payroll reconciliation needs the day somebody asks for one. Blank fields are left out of a person's summary rather than shown as a column of dashes. See [Contact details](https://github.com/edmozley/freeitsm/wiki/Contact-Details).

- **Your customers can keep their own details current.** A signed-in portal user opens **My Account** and can now maintain their **job title, office, phone and mobile** themselves, beside their preferred name. They are told, plainly, that only the IT team can see them. Department, employee ID and manager are deliberately not offered — nobody should be able to nominate their own approver, and a payroll number is a claim about who somebody is rather than a way of reaching them.

- **Where somebody's details come from elsewhere, they are read-only and say so.** Whether that is Active Directory, LDAP or now a CardDAV address book, the details are shown but cannot be edited — on both analyst screens and in the portal — and each says why. That is a deliberate refusal rather than a gap: the next import would overwrite anything typed locally, and an edit that quietly reverts overnight is how people conclude the system lost their change.

- **Copy and paste on the staff rota.** Right-click a cell to **Copy** the shift in it and right-click another to **Paste**. If the target already has something, you are asked before it is replaced.

- **Copy a whole week of the rota onto another week.** **Copy week** puts the week on the clipboard and a **Paste week** button appears, naming which week it is holding — by the time you have navigated three weeks away, a bare "Paste week" is a guess. Replacing a week that already has entries asks first, with numbers, and replace genuinely means replace rather than merge: *"This week has 5 entries already. Pasting replaces the whole week with the 5 you copied, so the 5 currently here are removed."* Nothing is lost quietly either — if a shift or a location has been retired since you copied, you are told how many entries could not be placed and why.

- **In-app help for six System screens that had none.** Service status on the portal, Search, Calendar sync, AI thinking, Integrations, and Date and time formats. The help index listed all six at the foot as "not yet documented"; it now offers a guide for every area. Each leads with the thing you would otherwise find out the hard way — that switching the status portal on publishes incident titles written for your team, that the calendar-sync permission reaches every mailbox in your tenant unless you scope it, and that a search for `VPN` finding nothing is your database declining to index short words rather than a broken index.

## Improvements

- **The people import can be put on a schedule, and the documentation finally says so.** `php scripts/directory_sync.php --all` picks up every enabled source — address books and directories alike — and sends each through the right engine. It has existed for the LDAP import since that shipped and was listed nowhere, so no operator could learn from the documentation that any of it could be automated. It is now on [Scheduled tasks](https://github.com/edmozley/freeitsm/wiki/Scheduled-Tasks) with the other jobs.

- **A person's preferred name is now visible to your analysts.** Somebody can tell the portal they would rather be called "Edwardo" than "Edward", and until now that reached nobody on your team — it was collected and then shown on no screen they ever open. It appears on the person's summary as **Prefers to be called**, and only when it actually differs from their name.

- **The System help pages now look like every other guide in FreeITSM.** They were built six weeks before the shared house style existed and were missed when everything else moved onto it, so they had their own layout, their own colours and no proper indenting — headings and body text sat at the same level on every page. They now use the same numbered sections, notes and tables as the module guides, and the help index is full width instead of a narrow column on a wide screen. Developers: [Help page house style](https://github.com/edmozley/freeitsm/wiki/Help-Page-House-Style).

- **Adding an address book reuses the Authentication screen properly.** Its dialogue keeps its title and buttons in place while the form scrolls, row actions are icons like every other settings screen, and the labels change to suit what you are adding — "Enabled" means "import contacts" for an address book, and the fields that only make sense for a sign-in method are not shown at all.

- **Pasting a rota week is one operation.** Seven analysts across seven days used to be up to 49 separate saves, where the first failure left half a week behind with no way to tell which half. It either looks like the week you copied, or it is untouched.

- **Directory-managed records say so before you try to edit them.** The note explaining that somebody's details come from your staff directory is now tinted and marked, rather than grey text easily read past — it is the only thing that explains why the boxes below will not accept typing.

## Fixes

- **Some dialogue boxes could not be scrolled, and their bottoms were unreachable.** Where a form grew taller than the window, **Save** and **Cancel** stayed pinned at the bottom as normal — so the box looked complete — while the last few fields sat below the visible area with no scrollbar to reach them. **New asset** in the asset module was affected, and so was the person editor this release adds fields to. The cause had been there since the current dialogue layout landed and only showed up once a form was long enough to overflow, so a third dialogue built the same way was one field away from it.

- **Deleting a sign-in method asked you to confirm, without saying what.** The confirmation box was completely blank — a bare "Confirm" with Cancel and OK and no question between them — so the only way to know what you were agreeing to was to remember what you had clicked. A second one like it, when attaching a ticket to an external tracker, is fixed too.

- **Saving your phone number in the portal wiped your preferred name.** Anything saved without also re-sending the preferred name cleared it — the name FreeITSM uses to address you in every email it sends. Deliberately clearing it still works; forgetting to mention it no longer does.

- **Pasting a rota week could have deleted entries nobody could see.** Replacing a week means clearing what the copied week did not have, and the obvious way to do that would also have removed shifts belonging to deactivated analysts, and shifts on days you have chosen not to display. The clearing is now scoped on the server to exactly what you were shown.

- **A person's manager could have been cleared without anyone seeing it happen.** On installations with more than one company, if somebody's manager was in a company you cannot see, the manager box appeared empty — and saving the page would have recorded "no manager", removing a reporting line you were never shown.

- **The portal's account dialogue was a sheet of white in dark mode.** **My Account** and **Multi-Factor Auth** had been written in fixed light colours, so anyone using the portal's dark theme got a glaring white panel with dark input boxes inside it. Both now follow the theme properly, along with the account dropdown behind them. The QR code for setting up MFA deliberately keeps its white background — a phone camera needs it to scan.

- **A portal user signed in through a staff directory was shown locked fields with no explanation.** The note telling them their details come from their organisation's directory was present but not being displayed at all, so the boxes simply refused to accept typing for no visible reason.

- **The help said approvals follow the manager chain. They do not.** Catalogue request approvals go to an analyst named on the form. The reporting line is recorded so it is ready if that changes, and the help now says that instead of promising something that does not happen.

- **Searching System for "address book" or "carddav" found nothing.** The Authentication card's search terms still listed only sign-in vocabulary, so the one screen that sets up an address book was reachable only by somebody who already knew where it was.

## Upgrading

1. Pull the new code, or rebuild the Docker image.
2. Sign in as an administrator and run **System → Database Verification**. **This one is required if you want the CardDAV import** — it adds seven columns to the authentication table. It changes nothing else and touches no existing data.
3. **Hard-refresh your browser (Ctrl-F5).** This matters more than usual: the dialogue-scrolling fix is in a stylesheet, so a browser holding the old one keeps the bug.

If you want the import to run on its own, add a scheduled task for `php scripts/directory_sync.php --all` — nightly is a sensible starting point. Nothing else to do: no new settings and no configuration changes.

## Rollback

Safe. The new columns are additions, nothing was removed from the database and no existing column changed shape, so you can go back to 1.7.0 without losing anything. An address book you had configured simply stops being read until you upgrade again; the contacts it imported stay exactly where they are, as ordinary people records.
