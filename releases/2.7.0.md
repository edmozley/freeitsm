## What this release is about

If you run FreeITSM in Docker, you can now turn on **HTTPS** without knowing anything about certificates. Type the name your people use to reach FreeITSM, such as `freeitsm.internal`, press Generate, and the screen walks you through the rest. It came from an email by somebody running FreeITSM in Docker who wanted the padlock but did not know where to start.

The rest of the release fixes two screens that shape what your customers see: the **Self-service portal** settings, which forgot what you had saved, and the **portal sign-in** tagline, which moved about. Both were reported in [#151](https://github.com/edmozley/freeitsm/issues/151) and [#150](https://github.com/edmozley/freeitsm/issues/150).

**Fixes an issue present in 2.5.0 and 2.6.0:** pressing Save on **System → Self-service portal** could switch your portal settings back to the defaults. If you use that screen, check it after upgrading - see Fixes below.

## New features

- **System → Docker: turn on HTTPS.** Only shown when FreeITSM runs in its Docker image. Enter the name people will type and the server's IP address, press **Generate**, and the page lists what is left to do: the DNS record (or hosts-file line) to add, a **Download** of the certificate to install on your PCs with steps for Windows, Group Policy, Intune and Mac, and the one command to restart the container. The page turns green once HTTPS is on. HTTPS is served on port 8443 by default.

  Public certificate services such as Let's Encrypt cannot issue certificates for private names like `.internal`, so FreeITSM makes a small certificate authority of its own for you to trust. It is restricted to the one name and IP you entered, so trusting it on your PCs does not let it vouch for any other website. A broken or missing certificate never stops the container starting: it stays on plain HTTP until the certificate is valid.

- **Two tagline lines on the sign-in pages.** **System → Branding** now has **Tagline 1** and **Tagline 2**. On the portal sign-in, Tagline 2 takes the place of *"Sign in to view your tickets"*, so you can word that line yourself. Leave it empty and nothing changes. On the staff sign-in it is simply a second line.

## Fixes

- **System → Self-service portal forgot its settings.** Every time the page opened it showed the defaults, the background pattern choices were missing until you pressed Save, and **pressing Save wrote those defaults over your real settings** - including switching off *Let people close their own ticket* and *Show people the equipment assigned to them*. Broken since 2.5.0. Your settings were stored correctly until then; the screen simply did not read them. **If you pressed Save on that screen in 2.5.0 or 2.6.0, open it after upgrading and put back anything that was lost.**

- **The portal sign-in tagline moved when you saved it.** While editing, the preview showed it below *"Sign in to view your tickets"*; once saved it appeared above, pressed against the title with no gap. The preview and the page now always agree, and the tagline has proper spacing.

- **A Remove button showed on System → Self-service portal** when there was no portal logo to remove.

## Improvements

- **This release's new screens are English-only in the other thirteen languages for now.** All thirteen complete languages still cover everything up to 2.5.0; the 56 strings added here, like 2.6.0's 35, fall back to English cleanly and will be translated in the next batch.

## Upgrading

1. Pull the new code, or rebuild the Docker image.
2. Sign in as an administrator and run **System → Database Verification**. Nothing changes in the database this time, but it is a good habit.
3. Hard-refresh your browser (Ctrl-F5).

**Docker installs:** HTTPS is optional, and nothing changes until you use System → Docker. The standard `docker-compose.yml` now publishes port 8443 and adds a `tls` volume for the certificate. If your compose file is your own, or older, the System → Docker page shows a short `docker-compose.override.yml` to add first, and will not make a certificate until it is in place. That is so the next update cannot delete a certificate your PCs have been told to trust.

## Rollback

Safe - nothing was removed from the database, so you can go back to 2.6.0 with the code alone.
