## What this release is about

Change Management can now follow your organisation's own risk policy instead of a fixed 5 × 5: your likelihood and impact levels and what each means, your results and their colours, and the result every combination gives. Changes you have already rated keep the scale they were rated on, so nothing you or an approver has seen changes underneath you. If you never set up a policy, nothing changes.

## New features

- **Your own risk policy.** **Change Management → Settings → Risk policy** lets you set 2 to 10 likelihood and impact levels, each with the criteria from your policy, 2 to 8 results with their colours, and the result for every cell of the matrix - four levels, five, or whatever your policy says. The criteria show in the **?** guide when someone rates a change, and the matrix on a change is drawn at your policy's size. Every save is a new version: a change keeps the version it was rated under, and **Re-assess** moves an older change onto the current one when you choose to. The built-in 5 × 5 stays the default. Asked for by Dr4inder.

## Improvements

- **Empty Procedures fold away too.** With **System → Preferences → Empty work sections on a task** set to fold, an empty Procedures section now shrinks to *Procedures (0)* like Checklists, Forms and Follow-ups. If procedures cannot load, the section now says so with **Retry** instead of disappearing, and a half-typed procedure search survives a refresh. Contributed by Dr4inder.

## Upgrading

1. Pull the new code, or rebuild the Docker image.
2. Sign in as an administrator and run **System → Database Verification**.
3. Hard-refresh your browser (Ctrl-F5).

To use your own risk policy, give whoever will set it up the new permission **Manage the change risk policy** (administrators already have it). If any workflow compares a change's risk with *Low*, *High* and so on, and your policy uses other words, update those conditions when you save it.

## Rollback

Safe - this release only adds to the database, so you can go back to 4.9.0. Changes rated on a policy of your own would then show their result without its colour, and re-rating one would use the 5 × 5 again.
