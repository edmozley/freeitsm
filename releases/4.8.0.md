## What this release is about

Background jobs - SLA warnings, webhooks, repeating tasks, directory imports and the rest - used to need a scheduled task each. Now one scheduled task runs them all, you choose when each runs inside FreeITSM, and the Docker image runs them with nothing to set up. Asset labels can be designed on any label size, including Brother and Dymo tape, with your logo where you want it. New tickets can be given a type the moment they arrive, People gains an organisation chart, and internal websites can have their certificates checked from inside your network.

## New features

- **One scheduled task for every background job.** Schedule `cron/run.php` to run every minute and it starts each job when it is due - including jobs added by later releases. **Reporting → System logs → Background jobs** says whether it is running and, when it is not, shows the exact line to schedule on your server. **Edit** on a job sets when it runs: every so many minutes, once a day at a set time, or off. The design came from vsc55.
- **Docker runs the background jobs by itself,** with nothing to set up on the host. Set `TZ` in `docker-compose.yml` so a daily time means your local time; the Background jobs page warns until you do. For installs that cannot have a scheduled task at all, there is an optional fallback that starts due jobs while somebody has FreeITSM open - off unless you switch it on.
- **Asset labels for label printers, with your logo large.** Under **Assets → Settings → Asset labels & QR**, keep several label designs on any size - Brother TZe and DK tapes, Dymo labels, the A4 sheets, or your own - including one with your logo large on the left and the QR code on the right. Labels download as a PDF at the label's exact size, one per page on a tape, for your printer's own driver. Names in any language print correctly, and you can upload your own fonts. Your current label carries on as the "Standard" design, so nothing changes until you choose. Asked for by zemega.
- **Design your own labels.** **Design** beside a label design opens a drag-and-drop designer that works like PowerPoint: place text, fields, the QR code, your logo, lines and boxes anywhere on the label, with snapping guides, alignment tools and undo. The label is drawn with a real asset's values - or a sample with very long names - so you can see what fits before you print.
- **Every new ticket can have a type.** Under **Tickets → Settings → Ticket types**, set a default type for new tickets, and rules before it - by how the ticket arrived (email, portal, web chat, phone, a monitoring alert...), its mailbox, origin, department, words in the subject or the requester's email domain. Emails to your requests mailbox can become service requests and monitoring alerts incidents, the moment they arrive. A type somebody chooses always wins, and **Try it** shows which type a ticket would get. Off until you set it. To change a type later in a ticket's life, Workflow has a new **Set ticket type** action. Suggested by duongtuanan.
- **An organisation chart.** **People → Organisation** shows who reports to whom, from the managers your directory already brings in: a tree you can open level by level, or a chart of boxes and lines, with the gaps - leavers who still manage people, and people with no manager. Export it as an image or as data. Contributed by vsc55.
- **Laptops assigned from Intune.** Switch on **Assign assets to their primary user in Intune** under **Assets → Settings → Intune**, and each asset nobody holds is given to its device's primary user at the next sync - matched exactly to the person imported from Entra ID, never by name. An asset that already has a holder is never changed. Off until you switch it on. Contributed by vsc55.
- **Certificates on internal websites, checked from inside.** On an internal domain's Health tab, list the websites to check - a name and a port, such as an intranet on 8443. FreeITSM reports each certificate expired, for the wrong name, untrusted or not answering, and warns as it nears expiry, through the same alerts and calendar entries as public domains. Certificates from your own certificate authority are checked against it once you add it as a trust profile. It only connects where you allow: under **Domains → Settings → Internal monitoring** you choose the networks (none until you tick one) and the ports, and setting it up needs the new **Internal monitoring** permission.

## Improvements

- **Help choosing a change's risk ratings.** A **?** beside Risk likelihood and Risk impact on a change opens a short guide: what each rating asks, what 1 to 5 might look like, and a worked example. Suggested by Dr4inder.

## Fixes

- **The risk level on a change (Low to Critical) was always in English,** whatever language you use FreeITSM in. It is now shown in your language. The risk matrix also coloured two cells as Medium that score as Low; it now matches.
- **A background job started twice at once - by an old scheduled task and a page, say - could run twice.** Every job now runs one at a time, whatever started it.
- **Deleting an analyst who had worked in some newer areas** (form approvals, 3CX calls, virtualisation reviews and a few others) did not account for that work. It is now kept and credited to them, as everywhere else.
- **On a Windows (WAMP) server, the Intune sync could fail to start** because FreeITSM picked the oldest version of PHP installed. It now uses the same PHP the web server runs.

## Upgrading

1. Pull the new code, or rebuild the Docker image.
2. Sign in as an administrator and run **System → Database Verification**.
3. Hard-refresh your browser (Ctrl-F5).

**Then, once: schedule `cron/run.php` to run every minute** (not needed on Docker). **Background jobs** shows the exact line for your server. **Already have a scheduled task for each job?** Nothing breaks: they keep working, and adding the new one alongside them is safe. Once **Background jobs** shows the new scheduler running, remove the old ones when it suits you. [Scheduled tasks](https://github.com/edmozley/freeitsm/wiki/Scheduled-Tasks#already-have-one-task-per-job) has the steps for Windows, Linux and Docker.

## Rollback

Safe - this release only adds to the database, so you can go back to 4.7.0. Your old scheduled tasks keep working either way; on Docker, 4.7.0 does not run the jobs by itself.
